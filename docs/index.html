01

Abstract

Accurate neural tissue segmentation is an important perception capability for navigation and robotic assistance in endoscopic spine surgery. The task is difficult because neural structures deform, have weak boundaries, and may be obscured by blood, surgical instruments, ligamentum flavum, and epidural fat. Vascularized disc tissue and specular reflections can also resemble neural tissue and cause clinically undesirable false positives.

DHN-Seg studies a high-precision static semantic teacher with explicit supervision for difficult positive and difficult negative evidence. The framework combines field-of-view-aware preprocessing, a general feature encoder and mask decoder, difficulty-balanced sampling, hard-pixel learning, prototype separation, and boundary supervision. This static model is designed both as a deployable frame-level recognizer and as a semantic anchor for future continuous-video segmentation.

Continuous-video pilot

Static accuracy does not automatically produce temporally stable video masks.

Six 24 FPS clips were used to compare framewise inference with several SAM2 propagation strategies. DINOv3-L + UPerNet produced the smoothest spatial boundary in qualitative review, while generic SAM2 required further domain adaptation. Patient-derived demonstrations remain available only on the team server.

Responsible release

The cohort is under active annotation and review. Patient-derived material will not be released until ethics approval, institutional authorization, and an appropriate data-governance plan are in place. Current results are preliminary and are not intended for clinical use.
